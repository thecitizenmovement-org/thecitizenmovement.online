Line intrusion & port-out check

Physical-signal detection needs hardware. What you can verify from the phone is below, in order of usefulness.

1. Port-out history. Ask your carrier for a port-out history and account audit. Unauthorized port requests are the single most common takeover path.
2. SIM swap signs. Sudden loss of service, missed calls you never saw ring, texts from accounts you didn't trigger, carrier says "device changed."
3. Interception signs. Battery drain that started abruptly, device warm at idle, echo or delay on calls, data use spikes. Note the date each first appeared.
4. Carrier account audit. Request authorized-user list, recent plan changes, and any device-financing or SIM orders you didn't make, in writing.
5. Reporting. Unauthorized porting goes to your carrier's fraud team and the FCC complaint portal. Keep the ticket numbers.

Footprint sweep routine

Run this in order, once a month. Log dates and results in the Case Log.

1. Search your name in quotes, plus city, plus employer, in three engines. Screenshot results with the date bar visible.
2. Search each email address and each phone number you have ever used, one at a time.
3. Check the four paid-broker clusters: people-search, background-check, marketing-data, and property-record aggregators. They resell each other, so a removal upstream cascades.
4. Check jail and court rosters separately. Those are public record and usually immune to broker-style opt-outs, but they can be corrected if wrong.
5. Archive before you erase. The snapshot is the evidence; the erasure is the remedy.
6. Keep a dated hash of your archive. If a site later claims it never held you, the snapshot settles it.

What it does not do

It does not identify a caller. No free service can. It gives you the structure of the number, a burner score, and the fastest route to each source that might know more.
It does not detect interception at the radio layer. That requires hardware. It gives you the paper trail to prove a port-out or SIM swap happened.
It does not scrape sites that forbid it, and it never logs into anything. Evidence gathered in violation of a site's terms is evidence opposing counsel gets to attack.
It does not replace a chain of custody. Screenshot with a visible clock, keep the raw file, and record the URL and time in the Case Log as you go.

Evidence hygiene, in four rules

1. Capture the whole page, not the interesting part. Context is what survives cross-examination.
2. Record the URL, the timestamp, and the device, at the moment of capture.
3. Never annotate the original file. Copy it, mark the copy.
4. Keep a hash list. This page can generate one on request for any file you pick.
